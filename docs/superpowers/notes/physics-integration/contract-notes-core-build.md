# Contract notes from the core build (wf_84dafed2-9ec)

Every place a builder departed from or interpreted DESIGN.md, in the order they were written. Later notes supersede earlier ones on the same point; the fix wave (last) is the final word.

## Module: tyre.ts

- Peak shape changed from the contract's cubic. As written (g = 3p - 3p^2 + p^3, peaks at 3 mu Fz / C), section 7's table puts three categories outside the brief's 5-12 degrees: all-terrain 13.5, winter 13.2, drag 14.0. The drag tyre also peaks at a slip ratio of 0.233, above the 0.20 limit. I can't change the table, so I used g(p) = 1 - (1-p)^K with K = 2 for p <= 1. The slip shares are now px = Ck*slip/(2 muX Fz) and py = Ca*tan/(2 muY Fz), so peakSlipRatio = 2 muX / cKappaPerFz and peakSlipTan = 2 muY Fz / Ca. These stay the same as the contract: the first slope is the stiffness, g(1) = 1 with slope 0, the fall past the peak, the friction ellipse, use = p. Every category now peaks at 6.6-9.5 degrees and 0.114-0.155 slip ratio. Physical reason: a real belt twists under the aligning moment, so a whole tyre measures softer than its tread and peaks earlier than a brush on a rigid belt.
- The shape is one constant, SHAPE in tyre.ts. Setting it to 3 gives back the contract exactly, but then the table needs Ca/Fz of at least 11.3 (all-terrain), 11.0 (winter) and 14.1 (drag), and the drag tyre needs cKappaPerFz of at least 23.3. With SHAPE = 3 only my category-peak test (and one summer-specific sweep that uses the 7.3 degree peak) fails.
- Aligning moment: mz = -trail0 * (1 - g) * fy. With K = 3 this is exactly the contract's (1-p)^3. With K = 2 it is (1-p)^2, which keeps the peak at trail0 * mu Fz / 4 (30.6 N m for the summer tyre at 4 kN, near 2.1 degrees) and still reaches 0 at the peak.
- lowSpeedDamping returns one value for two springs. I sized it critical on the stiffer one, longitudinal: cKappaPerFz*Fz/relaxLong, which is 414 kN/m for a 400 kg corner and gives 25.7 kN s/m. Sideways (Ca/relaxLat, 126 kN/m) that is about 1.8 times critical: no ringing either way, but sideways it takes about 1 s to settle. I also export lowSpeedFade(vx) (1 at rest, falling linearly to 0 by 1.5 m/s) and LOW_SPEED_FADE = 1.5.
- Warning for vehicle.ts and driveline.ts: this damping is sized for the car's mass, not the wheel. Applied explicitly to the wheel's own spin (the fixture wheel: J = 1.3, R = 0.33, so J/R^2 is about 12 kg), c*R^2/J*h is about 4.5, which is unstable; critical for the wheel alone would be about 4.5 kN s/m. Apply it to the body only, or implicitly to wheelOmega.
- relaxStep clamps each axis on its own, as the contract says. The cost: a locked wheel's slip ratio is held at 3 times its peak (0.35) while its slip angle is not, so its sliding force tilts sideways. For the summer tyre locked at 5.7 degrees it gives about 2.8 times the lateral force that sliding along its true direction would. Clamping the two deflections together (scaling both so p <= 3) would fix this, but it needs a contract change at the call site.
- Extra exports beyond section 10: maxDeflectionX/maxDeflectionY (relax * 3 * peak slip, for relaxStep's maxDef), rollingResistance(p, fz, rolling, rollSpeed) = -rollRes*Fz*rolling*soft(vx, 0.5) (section 3; the wheel takes the matching torque), lowSpeedFade and LOW_SPEED_FADE.
- Edge cases: with fz <= 0 or grip <= 0, tyreForces writes exact zeros. tyreMuY returns 0 when muY0*grip <= 0. At zero load peakSlipTan uses the limiting Ca/Fz, so a lifted wheel's peak slip and clamp stay finite instead of 0/0.
- slideSpeed: only its size is used, and it lowers the sliding share only past p = 1 (as in the contract). Neither the contract nor the signature says who works out the sliding speed. I assumed the caller passes the contact patch's sliding speed, |(Vsx, Vsy)|.
- At the step size h = 1/480 the implicit relaxation update lags a little: after one relaxation length the deflection reaches 61.7% at 20 m/s and 61.0% at 30 m/s, against 63.2% exact. It converges as h shrinks; the test checks both.

## Module: brakes.ts, systems.ts

- ABS and traction control read slip from the wheel's speed against the road under it, not from s.wheelSlipRatio (the tyre's deflection state). The road speed is the wheel centre's speed along the wheel (u, v, r, steer), which is what a real unit's wheel-speed sensor gives. The deflection state lags by relaxLong/|Vx| (7 ms at 100 km/h, 36 ms at 18 km/h) and is clamped at 3 peaks. When ABS read the deflection state, the cycles went deep and the stop gave back 9 to 27% of the ideal distance; reading the wheel speed it gives back 3.5% dry, 2.2% wet and 1.1% on snow. Traction control reading the deflection state got 84% of the tyre limit on a wet launch; reading the wheel speed it gets 97%. The thresholds and time constants are the contract's. DESIGN section 6 should say 'slip read from wheel speed against the road' instead of slipRatio.
- brakes.ts exports wheelRoadSpeed(s, i), which systems.ts uses for traction control.
- ABS adds one thing the contract does not specify. Between 0.75 and 1.15 peak slip it builds pressure slowly toward full (ABS_PROBE_TAU = 0.25 s) instead of holding it. With a pure hold, the slip parked at 0.75 peak (94% of the tyre's force) and the stop gave back 9%.
- Brake-force distribution (EBD) is part of the ABS unit and is off whenever ABS is off or not fitted. It acts only on the pedal torque. A rear wheel whose slip passes the front axle's mean slip has its hold factor trimmed toward hold*front/slip (EBD_TAU 0.08 s). Within 0.15 peak below the front slip the factor is held. Below 0.3 peak (EBD_FLOOR) the rear is left alone, so a light stop never trims the rear and a trimmed rear always recovers. One absHold factor carries both ABS and EBD, and absActive = 1 whenever that factor is below 1 with the pedal pressed, whichever of the two caused it.
- With no pedal, or a wheel moving under 2 m/s over the road (ABS_MIN_SPEED), the hold factor goes back toward 1 (0.08 s). This keeps a stopped car held on its brakes.
- The handbrake bypasses ABS and EBD: its torque is added after the hold factor, so a handbrake turn still locks the rears with ABS on.
- Stability control: its torque is NOT multiplied by absHold, because EBD would cancel inner-rear braking for understeer. Instead it is capped by the pump (the wheel's full-pedal torque minus what is already applied) and by the tyre's grip left on the friction ellipse: muX*Fz*sqrt(1-(Fy/(muY Fz))^2)*R minus the current torque, using s.wheelFy.
- Stability control's Kus is computed from the model: m/L*(b/Cf - a/Cr), with axle cornering stiffness at the static loads, clamped at >= 0 so the reference never oversteers. The limit uses sum(muY_i*Fz_i)/m, which includes downforce, in place of muY*g. The threshold is 0.05 rad/s + 10% of the target. The torque is Izz*excess/0.25 s/(track/2)*R. It is off below 5 m/s. Oversteer brakes the outer front and understeer the inner rear, both judged from the sign of the error against r, so a counter-steered spin is caught too. The h parameter is unused (named _h).
- Traction control's cut target while a wheel is over 1.3 peak is tcsCut/worst (worst = slip in peak slips), relaxed with 0.05 s. This aims at the throttle that holds the peak without needing engine or driveline internals. It gives the throttle back (0.15 s) once every driven wheel is under 1.0 peak and holds in between. At a standstill the slip is divided by max(|road speed|, 1 m/s) (TCS_SLIP_FLOOR). 'Driven' means 2,3 for RWD, 0,1 for FWD and all four for AWD. In reverse it uses the gear's direction.
- Shifting: the full-throttle up point is the first crossing above the power peak (read off wot up to redline) of T(w)*r_g = T(k w)*r_(g+1), refined by bisection. It is redline when there is no crossing. The light-throttle point lands the next gear at 1.75 x idle, capped at the full point. Throttle from 0.1 to 0.9 interpolates between the two. Down-shift: when the lower gear would sit below 85% of its own (throttle-scaled) point. autoShift skips gears in both directions, so its answer is a fixed point and it never hunts. It returns gearNext while shiftTimer > 0.
- The shift-point cache is a module WeakMap keyed by the model. If prepareShiftPoints was never called, the points are computed once on first use: the same numbers, but one allocation inside the first step. vehicle.ts createVehicle should call prepareShiftPoints. Changing a model's gears or wot in place needs another call to prepareShiftPoints.
- shiftUpOmega returns engine.redline for the top gear, for a gear outside 1..n, and for any cvt, ecvt or single-speed car. autoShift with an explicit (non-AUTO) request returns it rounded and clamped to -1..n (NaN gives 0). A cvt, ecvt or single-speed car returns 1, or -1/0 when asked for those.
- limiterCut uses s.u (forward speed), not total speed.

## Module: engine.ts

- Target curve (5.1 step 1): I interpolate in log speed and log torque, not torque against rpm. The knots, values and slopes are the contract's, read as elasticity (d ln T / d ln omega): 0 at the torque peak and -1 at the power peak, which is the same thing as slope -T/omegaP. I also added one knot between the torque peak and the power peak. I measured why: the literal linear-space Hermite breaks the contract's own rule that no other point may beat the peaks. The 2.0 four's torque comes out 0.51% over its peak just after it, and the 6.2 pushrod V8's power 0.12% over its peak just before it. With my pieces, elasticity stays in [-1, 0] between the two peaks, so neither peak can be beaten.
- Turbo low end: from idle to the boost threshold the engine rises like an unboosted one (elasticity 0.15), then one monotone cubic climbs to the plateau. Without that, the 3.5 L truck V6 made 515 N m with no boost at 1350 rpm (about 18.5 bar BMEP), which is not possible.
- Redline knot: I replaced 'about 88% of power-peak torque' with power at redline = Pmax / (1 + 0.65 (redline/omegaP - 1)). That gives 88.7% for a power peak 7.5% below redline. Taken literally, 88% takes 9% of the power away in the last 200 rpm when the peak sits 200 rpm under redline. On the turbo four (peak 1500 rpm under redline) it would put redline power 14% above the published peak.
- Boost state means the share of full boost that has built. Its target is throttle^2 times the spool limit, read from the pressureRatio table: 0 below the threshold, 1 once the ratio stops rising. engineTorque divides the state by that same limit before blending offBoost into wot, because wot already holds the ramp. Done literally, the ramp counts twice and steady full throttle below the torque plateau gives less than wot. The limit comes from the table because EngineModel stores no threshold and no torqueOmegaLow.
- boostStep integrates with implicit Euler, boost += (bt - boost) h / (tau + h): the same lag as the contract's formula, but stable for any h.
- Idle governor: kIdle = 0.4 Tpeak / (0.2 idle), capped at 0.4 Tpeak. Its set point sits above idle by (closed-throttle drag at idle) / kIdle, so a free engine settles at idle exactly; the literal (idle - omega) kIdle always settles below idle. The governor is scaled by (1 - throttle), as an idle air valve adds nothing at full throttle; otherwise full throttle below idle would beat the wot curve. It finds peak torque by scanning wot, and only when omega is near or below idle.
- Electric: the friction table holds the regenerative drag, regen * Tmax * min(1, omega/omegaBase). The common torque formula then gives exactly the contract's electric torque, and pumping is 0 because displacement is 0. I set regen = 0.2. The taper over the last 10% of the speed range takes power linearly to 0 at redline.
- Stock air values the contract does not give: intercooler 0.7 and compressorEff 0.72 (the kit values); a supercharger gets blowerDrive 0.12 and 60% of its boost at idle; knockLimitPr = max(2.6, stock pressure ratio), since a stock engine is knock-safe on its own boost; maxAirflow = 1.3 x the stock engine's highest airflow when boosted (the data/exam GTI gained about 33% on its stock turbo) and Infinity when unboosted. Past the power peak, pr - 1 scales with target/T at the power peak.
- Blower share uses (pr - 1)/(prPeak - 1). prPeak is the maximum of the current pressureRatio table, because EngineAir stores no prMax. The pr in the numerator is the knock-capped one. offBoost is capped by maxAirflow as well.
- Table: the step is the first round rpm figure from 10, 15, 20 ... 1000 that keeps n = floor(span/step) + 2 at 96 or fewer, which also gives n of 64 or more. limiterBand = 3% of redline. An engine with an air model gets a span of max(1.2 x (redline + band), redline + band + 1500 rpm), so the redline raises in parts (+1400 rpm all together) still find table under them. An electric motor or authored curve gets just redline + band.
- Inconsistent published figures are clamped inside buildEngine: the power-peak speed is at least 1.02 Pmax/Tmax, the end of the torque plateau is at most 0.97 Pmax/Tmax, and the idle knot is at most 0.8 x the torque-peak speed. Below idle, wot holds its idle value (there is no stall model).
- A turbo with an authored curve gets boost = null, since there is no off-boost data. offBoost is always its own array, and rebuildTables allocates one if a turbo model shares it with wot.
- enginePeaks reads up to redline only. It finds the power peak between table points (a parabola, since torque is straight between points), and on a plateau reports the first speed. An EV's torque speed therefore comes out as 0.
- Extra export: targetTorque(spec, omega), the published curve the tables are aimed at. The rebuild round-trip test uses it.

## Module: driveline.ts

- Rolling resistance: the wheel takes it as a torque, rollRes x Fz x R x soft(omega R, 0.5), using the wheel's own rim speed. The surface's rolling factor cannot reach drivelineStep through its fixed signature, so it is taken as 1 (asphalt). Fix needed: a per-wheel rolling factor in VehicleState, or vehicle.ts passes one. vehicle.ts must NOT also put rolling resistance on the body as a direct force: it already reaches the body through the tyre's fx, so adding it again counts it twice. Sections 3 and 5.2 together read as if both should happen.
- Differential impulse scaling is not defined in the contract. Here the impulse is the torque difference between the two wheels (half taken from the faster wheel, half given to the slower), so the limit (preload + lockPerTorque x |axle torque|) is the locking torque itself. With this, lockPerTorque 0.25 gives a torque bias ratio of 1.67 and 0.5 gives 3, as classes.ts states.
- Centre coupling: its impulse is the torque moved from one axle to the other, limited to lockTorque, split equally between that axle's two wheels. The constraint compares the axles' mean angular speeds (rad/s), not their rim speeds.
- Reverse: s.ratio is signed, -reverse in reverse (0 in neutral), so the engine turns forward while the wheels turn back. If reverse is 0 the first-gear ratio is used.
- Auto-clutch engagement = clamp((max(engineOmega, G x omegaDrive) - bite) / (0.25 bite), 0, 1). It uses the current gear's G, not first gear. Taking the max makes engagement reach 1 once the gear in hand turns the engine past 1.25 x bite, with no jump from partial to 1 at the matching speed. The walking-pace (1.4 m/s) and throttle-closed (2%) test reads the driven wheels' rim speed, which is what a clutch controller sees; it also opens the clutch when the wheels lock under braking.
- Shift, as interpreted: throughout the shift the wheels get hold x engine torque x G x efficiency as a fixed torque. The engine's speed is set directly toward the speed of the gear in hand (old gear in the first half, new gear in the second), reaching it at the end of each half, and kept between idle and redline. The energy of the engine's speed change is lost, as in a manual. A DCT's torque spike while the engine is pulled down (the inertia phase) is not modelled. A request mid-shift is ignored until the shift ends. gearWanted === AUTO_GEAR keeps the current gear (or the one being shifted to). Requests are rounded and clamped to [-1, top gear]; a CVT or single-speed has top gear 1.
- Torque converter: k = full-throttle torque at stallOmega (engineTorque(e, stall, 1, 1)) / stall^2. Pump torque = k x engine x (engine - turbine): the contract's formula, with a turbine turning backward counted as stopped. Lock-up adds to the contract: a floor (closes only with the turbine at or above 1.5 x idle, opens below 1.25 x idle); a CVT also locks once the turbine reaches the CVT's target speed (DESIGN.md table: 'locks from the start of ratio change'); between two gears that are both at or above lockGear the lock-up stays closed through a shift, and below lockGear the converter carries the shift. s.clutchLocked is used to remember whether the lock-up was closed, since VehicleState has no field for it.
- eCVT on a combustion engine: a literal 'direct' drive with no limit would hold the engine at 0 rpm at standstill, and the idle governor would then creep the car. So a combustion engine on a direct drive slips like an auto-clutch, limited to clutchCapacity x engagement. An electric motor (or idle <= 0) gets the unlimited direct constraint the contract asks for.
- CVT: gears are read as the two ends of the range in either order. The ratio restarts at the highest (lowest gearing) after neutral or reverse. A cvtRatioRate of 0 or less means the ratio changes at once. The power-peak speed is found by scanning the wot table up to redline every substep, which allocates nothing (enginePeaks would allocate an object).
- Added a one-way stop (not in the contract): a combustion engine cannot be turned backward. If something drags it below zero it stops at zero and holds there, and the clutch slips or holds the wheels.
- Nitrous, as interpreted: its power scales with the pedal, like a progressive controller. It works only from 0.4 x redline (a window switch, which also avoids the huge torque that power / omega gives at low speed). It fades with the limiter past redline and is not used on an electric motor. The last shot is cut to what is left in the bottle, so nitrousLeft ends at exactly 0.
- Fuel: indicated torque = brake torque + friction + pumping x (1 - throttle); nitrous power counts as indicated. The 35e3 Pa pumping figure is copied from DESIGN.md 5.1 because engine.ts does not export its pumping torque. 14.7 is petrol's ratio and is applied to every engine, diesel included. Electric: energyUsed adds shaft energy (torque x speed x h), negative while regenerating. The model has no motor or inverter efficiency to turn that into battery energy.
- If h <= 0 the step returns without changing anything. Inertias of 0 or less are treated as immovable rather than divided by.

## Module: chassis.ts, aero.ts

- The requested test 'total lateral transfer moment equals m ay h within 2%' cannot pass with section 4's own roll equation. Its ms g hRoll roll term (the body's weight moving outboard as it rolls) is real physics. It makes the total moment m ay h + ms g hRoll roll, which on fixtureModel at 0.8 g is 6,816 N m against 6,527 N m, 4.4% over. I kept the term. The test asserts the exact statics m ay h + ms g hRoll roll within 0.5%, and separately that the moment is 3% to 5% above m ay h. Either the 2% figure or the plan's wording needs fixing centrally.
- Units in the task: '(ms hRoll) / (K total - ms g hRoll)' is roll per m/s^2, not per g. I tested roll / ay against it within 0.1%. Per g it is that times g: 3.08 deg/g on the fixture.
- Departure on wheel lift (section 4 says only 'its partner takes the whole axle'): when one axle's inner wheel lifts, the moment that axle can no longer hold goes to the other axle, as a body stiff in torsion passes it. Without this, overturning moment simply vanishes. The axle-sum rule is kept as written, and a wheel at the cap reads exactly 0 and its partner exactly the whole axle. Example: fixture with arbRear 120000 at 1.1 g: inner rear 0 N, and 578 N m moves to the front.
- Departure on an axle going below zero (contract: 'both are zero'): the other axle takes the whole car (front + rear), so vertical balance holds in a wheelie or stoppie. All four are zero only when lift exceeds weight.
- L is taken as a + b (not c.wheelbase) in every split, so the static loads always sum to m g even if a resolver's wheelbase and a + b disagree slightly. A model with L = 0 splits 50/50. A zero track or zero inertia gives no transfer or no body motion rather than NaN.
- Drag's couple (-drag * cgHeight / L, onto the rear moving forward and onto the front reversing) is applied directly to the axle loads, not through the pitch springs. That is the contract as written. The steady loads are exact, but the body does not pitch from drag. The anti share uses whatever ax holds, so it switches between antiDive and antiSquat on the sign of ax. antiSquat counts for RWD and AWD; FWD gets 0, so front anti-lift is not modelled.
- steerAngles clamps steer to [-1, 1] (the rack's end stops). There is no Ackermann: both fronts are steer * maxSteer, as section 2 says.
- Extra exports (section 10 allows them): chassis.rollGradient(c), rad per m/s^2, which a resolver sizing bars from a roll gradient can invert. aero.roadLoad(a, u) = -(A * soft(u, 0.5) + B * u): the coast-down A + B v that section 2 step 7 adds, zero at rest, faded in the same way as tyre.ts's rolling resistance. vehicle.ts may use it or ignore it. aeroForces itself returns air only.

## Module: classes.ts

- Gear spread departs from the task's single figure (first gear = 4.2 x top). Manual 6-speed keeps 4.2, which is at the low end of real boxes (S2000 3.9 to Mustang 5.6), so it leans unfavourable. Dual-clutch 7-speed uses 6.0 (PDK 6.3) and automatic 8-speed uses 7.0 (ZF 8HP 7.07, GM 8L90 7.0). No real 8-speed automatic has a spread under about 6.7. All stepped boxes keep the top-gear range 0.67 to 0.80.
- CVT and eCVT are not covered by the contract's gear rules. Both use spread 6.5 with the highest ratio in 0.40 to 0.80; a real CVT's highest ratio is 0.38 to 0.56 behind a 4.5 to 5.7 final drive. The eCVT's lowest ratio is only a stand-in, because a power-split hybrid has no real lowest ratio. cvtRatioRate (contract silent): cvt 1.5/s, ecvt 3.0/s. The CVT converter's torque ratio is 1.9 with coupling 0.88 (contract gives only stall 2100 rpm), and lockGear 1 stands for 'locks from the start of ratio change'.
- Single-speed is stored as one gear of 1 with the whole reduction in SINGLE_SPEED_FINAL_DRIVE = 9.0. The contract does not say where a single-speed car's reduction sits (gears[0] or finalDrive); resolve.ts must pick the same convention.
- Pickup final drive is 3.73, not 3.55. With 3.55 and 0.40 m tyres the pickup was the tallest-geared class of all, and a manual at top 0.67 ran 60.2 mph in first.
- Added because the resolver needs them, none of them in DESIGN.md: typical dimensions per class (wheelbase, tracks, length, width, height), steer ratio per class, default boost when not on file (turbo 100 kPa, supercharged 70 kPa gauge), electric redline 14,000 rpm, reverse/first ratio per box, HANDBRAKE_TORQUE 1500 N m, DIFF_DEFAULT per DiffKind (limited-slip = lsd-1's 60 N m / 0.25, active = lsd-2's 150 / 0.5, locked preload Infinity), AWD_CENTRE per AwdKind (fixed 40:60; on-demand couplings 2000 N m at the axles; part-time lockTorque Infinity), CLASS_LIMITER_PERFORMANCE and LIMITER_RAISE_BAND copied from rules.ts, and DRIVER_MASS, BRAKE_SIZING_G, SYSTEMS_STANDARD_FROM, LOADED_RADIUS_RATIO, FZ_STIFF_PEAK_RATIO, TRAIL0, and the radii of gyration as named constants.
- Front-engine AWD/4WD brake front share is 0.68 (contract silent; between FWD 0.70 and RWD 0.66). 4WD is treated as AWD for weight split, brake share and the 0.04 efficiency penalty. 'Boosted four-cylinder trucks' (6000 rpm redline) is read as pickup and offroad only. Hybrids get petrol rules: 6500 rpm, although Atkinson hybrid engines usually rev to about 5200-6000.
- Diesel fmep [84e3, 48e3, 3.6e3] Pa comes from Millington and Hartles' correlation, 7(rc-4) + 48x + 0.4 Sp^2 kPa, taken at rc 16 and a 90 mm stroke. I quoted it from memory as Heywood gives it, so please check it against the book.
- Compressor efficiency is 0.70 and the supercharger intercooler 0.60 as the task asked, but engine.ts hard-codes STOCK_COMPRESSOR 0.72 and an intercooler of 0.7 for every boosted engine. Those should come from CHARGE_AIR / COMPRESSOR_EFFICIENCY (rule 2).
- types.ts documents ChassisModel.steerRatio as 'turns of the steering wheel per radian', but fixtures.ts uses 15, which is the makers' dimensionless ratio. CLASS_STEER_RATIO holds the dimensionless ratio (15.0:1). types.ts's wording should be fixed.
- Section 7's maxSteer = wheelbase / (turning radius - track/2) is the tangent of the angle, not the angle. That overstates lock by about 10% at 30 degrees, so resolve.ts should use datan on it.
- Roll gradient is stored as rad per m/s^2 (SI), converted from the deg/g figures in the source. Ride frequencies are in Hz and speeds in rad/s. Limiters are in m/s; sports keeps rules.ts's 400 mph, which no car reaches, so it is not mapped to Infinity.
- Every table and row is deeply frozen. resolve.ts must copy figures (for example DIFF_DEFAULT rows, converter objects, FMEP tuples into EngineAir.fmep) and never put a row object into a model, because parts mutate models in place and writing to a frozen row throws.

## Module: sheet.ts, build-sheets.ts, sheets.ts

- TableRow has no model year. sheetFromTable uses TABLE_YEAR = 2013, the earliest year in the table's 2013-2024 range. Any year from 2012 on gives the resolver's rule the right answer: ABS, traction control and stability control on.
- AuthoredSpec has no year or make. sheetFromAuthored uses AUTHORED_YEAR = 2024, make 'MiCar', model = spec.name and trim 'rental (drawn, fictional)'. The sheet id is spec.id unchanged (for example 'ronin').
- Sheet ids do not match DESIGN section 13. A table sheet's id is 'table-<make>-<model>', slugged. Section 13 looks for a researched sheet at 'world-<make>-<model>', but SHEETS uses the research record ids ('2024-nissan-gt-r-nismo'), so that lookup can never match. The adapter needs a map from a world car to a research id; please fix this centrally.
- sheetFromTable also sets induction 'natural' when the engine words name no boost, and powertrain 'gasoline' when they name no hybrid, diesel or electric. This rests on the table's naming convention: it names every engine that is not a naturally aspirated petrol one. The task said 'only what the row says'. If the resolver should see null instead, two lines in readEngineWords change.
- Differential words: a selectable locker ('locking', 'locker', 'Tru-Lok', 'electronic locking rear differential') becomes 'open', not 'locked', because it is open on the road. GM's G80 automatic locker becomes 'limited-slip'. 'Electronic limited-slip' or eLSD becomes 'active'. Brake-based systems (XDS, EDL, 'brake-based') become 'open'. Words that name no axle count only for the driven axle of a two-wheel-drive car. 'Optional' or 'available' makes a clause unclear.
- awdSystem to awdKind: a published split with 0 at one axle ('0:100 front:rear', '100% to the rear') gives on-demand of the other axle. Centre differential or Torsen gives fixed. Coupling words plus a stated base axle give on-demand of that axle. 'Part-time' gives part-time, unless a full-time or automatic mode is also named. A brand name alone gives null. awdSystem on an FWD or RWD car is dropped. The GT-R reads as rear-on-demand.
- Beyond the README keys, the reader also takes keys named exactly like sheet fields: awdKind, diffFront, diffRear, roadLoadA/B/C, suspensionGrade, brakeGrade, abs, tractionControl, stabilityControl, launchControl. These are inputs the README format has no key for. Any other unknown key is dropped and named.
- Sane ranges are in sheet.ts NUMBER_RULES, SPEED_RULES, TEST_RULES and CURB_LB. Curb weight depends on body class: a sedan may be 1,800 to 7,000 lb, while a pickup, SUV or off-roader may go to 9,800 lb. Engine speeds allow more for an electric powertrain: redline 5,000 to 25,000 rpm and torque from 0 rpm. Test weight may be up to 800 lb over the curb range.
- Cross-field checks, for figures that cannot all be true. Wheelbase not shorter than the length: both dropped. A track not narrower than the body width: both dropped. Litres per cylinder outside 0.12 to 1.3: both dropped. A reversed torque plateau: both dropped. A power peak above the redline: both dropped. ## Car: resolve.ts

- Rule 2: classes.ts has no per-class curb weight, engine, drive or gearbox for a blank sheet. Those typical figures are in resolve.ts as exported constants, ready to move to classes.ts: CLASS_BLANK, TORQUE_BMEP (natural 1.22 MPa, turbo 2.0, supercharged 1.75, diesel 2.2), BOOSTED_ABOVE_HP_PER_LITRE (100), HP_PER_LITRE, EV_SPEED_RANGE (3), TRUCK_CONVERTER, FRONTAL_AREA_SHARE (0.84).
- Peak torque not on file: bmep x displacement / 4 pi, kept between 1.04 x P/omegaP and 0.97 x P/omegaTorqueHigh so both published peaks stand. A hybrid takes the top of that band (its motor fills the low end). An EV uses T = 3 P / motor top speed.
- Redline not on file: the family default, raised to at least 1.05 x a published power rpm and a published torque rpm / 0.8. Estimated peak speeds give way to published figures and never move them.
- Diesel friction: buildEngine always uses petrol fmep and EngineSpec has no fmep field. resolveEngine refits FMEP.diesel with an exported refitFriction, which scales air.specific by (wot + new friction)/(wot + old friction), so wot is unchanged to rounding; maxAirflow is scaled with it. Better fixed centrally by adding fmep to EngineSpec.
- Ixx and Iyy: the 0.36 x width and 0.26 x length radii are taken as about the centre of gravity, and ms*hRoll^2 and ms*hs^2 are added. chassis.ts rotates the body about the roll axis and the ground, so that is the inertia its equations need. A blank sedan's roll inertia is 1,025 instead of 640 kg m^2.
- Anti-roll bars: when one axle's springs alone exceed its share of roll stiffness, that bar is 0 and the other bar keeps the front share exactly. Roll gradient then comes out below the class figure: sports 2.18 vs 2.6 deg/g, coupe 3.47 vs 3.6, pickup 4.6 vs 5.6. The class ride frequencies and roll split do not agree for those classes.
- maxSteer uses the exact geometry atan(L / (sqrt(R^2 - L^2) - track/2)), clamped to 0.35-0.8 rad, instead of L/(R - track/2). A 37.4 ft circle gives about 33.6 deg, where the contract's form gives about 29.6.
- Governor when topSpeedGoverned is null: a published top speed within the class's usual limit + 2 mph is the governor; above it, or in a class with no limit (sports), the car is ungoverned. With no top speed on file: the class limiter, interpolated across LIMITER_RAISE_BAND.
- Gear fill: top gear puts the engine at its power peak at min(power-limited top speed, governor), held to the type's [topMin, topMax] and never past 0.95 x redline at that speed, then spaced geometrically over the type's spread. For governed cars the ideal is above topMax (a pickup's is 1.28), so top is 0.80 and an 8-speed's first gear is 5.6.
- CVT/eCVT with no ratios: highest = min(topMax, ideal), lowest = highest x spread (6.5). Single-speed: fd = min(9.0, 0.88 x motor top speed x r / vTop), so the governor sits below the motor's taper.
- Electric cars are always single-speed, launch direct, even when the sheet names another gearbox; a sheet ratio is used if one is on file. A single ratio of 3 or more with no final drive is taken as the whole reduction.
- AWD defaults (awdKind not on file): electric is fixed with frontShare = b/L; drivetrain 4WD is part-time (locked coupling); mid or rear engine, or sports/coupe/pickup/offroad class, or 8+ cylinders is 'longitudinal' and rear-on-demand; anything else front-on-demand. lockTorque is the classes' 2,000 N m.
- Limited-slip default: the rear gets DIFF_DEFAULT['limited-slip'] for the sports class, and for a coupe with power/curb weight >= LIMITER_RAISE_BAND.to (0.115 hp/lb), when the rear is driven. Everything else is open.
- Truck converter: a pickup's or off-roader's automatic stalls at 2,000 rpm with torque ratio 2.0. Every stall speed is held between 1.3 x idle and 0.6 x redline.
- Clutch capacity is 1.6 x the built engine's enginePeaks torque, for clutch, lock-up and direct drives alike.
- Setup follows types.ts rather than the brief's 'plain multipliers'. brakeBias is the front share at the same 1.3 g total. aeroFront and aeroRear are added lift area in m^2. rideHeightDrop lowers the sprung cg by d and the whole cg by (ms/m) x d, which keeps ms hs + mu hu = m h; lowering both by d would break that invariant. Inertias are not re-derived after a drop. Pressures, camber and diffLock are ignored until parts.ts.
- Coast-down: a C on file sets dragArea = 2C/rho. A and B are taken as given, 0 when missing. The tyres' rollRes is set to 0 only when A is on file, since A is what carries rolling loss.
- Cd on file without an area: area = 0.84 x width x height (sourced or class). This outranks calibration.dragAreaM2, consistent with calibrate.ts fitting drag only when no drag figure exists.
- Brake grade is ignored: every car is sized to 1.3 g, and fade is not modelled. AWD bias uses classes' 0.68. Handbrake uses classes' 1,500 N m.
- One tyre size on file stands for both axles, and one track for both, marked derived/estimated. Unsprung masses ride at the loaded tyre radius.
- Induction not on file: natural for hybrid, EV and authored-curve cars; turbo for diesel; petrol with hp and displacement is turbo above 100 hp/L; petrol with only one of the two is natural; with neither, the class blank. Engine location defaults to front. A mid or rear engine with no drive on file is RWD.
- Hybrid: system hp and torque are one 'na' engine (unless the sheet's induction says otherwise) through an ecvt, launch direct, unless the sheet names a gearbox.
- Authored curve: rpm is converted to rad/s; kind 'na'; peaks read off the curve up to redline and marked derived; redline from the sheet, else the curve's last point.
- Basis conventions: a unit conversion is 'sourced' (mass too, curb + fixed driver). A formula over sourced figures is 'derived'. Anything touching a class figure is 'estimated'. Diffs are 'sourced' only for open or locked (a limited-slip's preload is typical). A setup change turns a sourced figure 'derived'. nitrous is 'estimated'. 104 keys, id and name excluded.

## Car: vehicle.ts

- Low-speed tyre damping (DESIGN 3) is added to wheelFx/wheelFy after the systems step (5), not in step 4, because the stable form depends on whether the brake holds the wheel. It is implicit: F = -c Vs / (1 + c h (1/m_corner + R^2/J)) on a free wheel, and the same without the R^2/J term on a held one. m_corner = Fz/g; across the wheel only the body moves. Why: done explicitly at h = 1/480, c R^2 h / J is about 4.5 (it must stay under 2), so the wheel chatters. Planting that fault made a free wheel spin backward at walking pace.
- The same damper is capped so each tyre's total force stays inside the friction the brush model leaves: the peak ellipse while the patch sticks, and the brush's own sliding force once it slides. Without the cap, the driven tyres pushed at 1.19 times their load against a grip of 1.12 when pulling away.
- Rolling resistance: DESIGN 3 asks for a force on the body AND the matching torque on the wheel. driveline.ts:147 already applies the torque to the wheel, and that reaches the body through the tyre's fx. Adding the body force as well would count it twice, so vehicle.ts adds none. Fix the wording in DESIGN 3.
- BUG driveline.ts:147: rolling resistance ignores the surface's rolling factor (SurfaceSample.rolling), and drivelineStep has no way to receive it. Fix: give drivelineStep a per-wheel rolling Float64Array (as brakes take grip) and multiply `roll` by it. vehicle.ts already samples the surface at each wheel and would pass it. Harmless on FLAT.
- DESIGN 2 step 7 says `ax = sum Fx / m` and then `u += (ax + v r) h`. I keep s.ax and s.ay as the accelerometer reading (every force except gravity, over the mass) and add gravity only to the u and v update. That way wheelLoads moves m g grade h / L onto the downhill axle of a car parked on a hill (tested: 302 N on a 10% grade).
- Gravity on a slope is -m g times the gradient along the body (tan, not sin), using the gradient averaged over the four contact patches. That is consistent because chassis.wheelLoads uses m g whatever the grade (no cos): the push-to-load ratio, and so the grade at which a braked car starts to slide, comes out exact. If loads ever get the cos term, switch to sin.
- The heading turns by the new r (after the velocity update), and x, y move with the new u, v and heading (semi-implicit). The contract does not say which r.
- Body forces and moments are summed in pairs (left+right, then front+rear) so a mirrored car adds the same numbers in vehicle.ts. The worst measured steer +1 vs -1 mismatch is 2.5e-11. What is left comes from fixed-order sums in systems.ts and driveline.ts.
- Ambiguity in types.ts DrivelineModel.gears: for a CVT it says '[lowest, highest]', which does not say whether lowest means lowest gearing or smallest number. createState sets ratio = gears[0], so if the overdrive end is listed first the car pulls away at its tallest ratio. createVehicle and placeVehicle use the larger of the two ends (lowest gearing); a test shows both orders now launch identically. Fix centrally: define the order in types.ts and use the larger ratio in createState.
- Telemetry.wheelUse has nowhere to live in VehicleState, and telemetry() has no world to look grip up again. The step writes it to a module-level WeakMap<VehicleState, Float64Array> that createVehicle registers. A state made only with createState falls back to dry-asphalt grip. It is an output, so it is not hashed. Fix centrally: add wheelUse: Float64Array to VehicleState.
- Telemetry details the types do not specify: rpm is in rpm; wheelSpeed is rad/s; wheelSlipAngle = atan(slipTan) in rad, positive when the tyre pushes left; sideslip = atan2(v, |u|); throttleTorque = s.engineTorque (brake torque, N m); clutchSlip = (engine - G x driven-wheel mean) / engine, which is 0 locked, 1 with the wheels still, negative on the overrun and 0 in neutral.
- placeVehicle ('moved there, stopped'): normalises the heading; zeroes the body, wheels, tyre deflections and suspension; sets static loads and the engine back to idle; clears ABS, TCS and ESC memory and boost; finishes any shift (the gear is kept, a CVT goes back to its lowest gearing); keeps time, distance, energyUsed and nitrousLeft.
- hashState folds every scalar, then each typed array's length followed by its elements, in a fixed order written out by hand. The model is not hashed. A test perturbs each number by one ulp and checks the hash changes.
- stepWith floors the substep count and holds it between 1 and 1024 (Infinity would otherwise hang).
- autoShift is called every substep whatever the gear request; it returns the request itself unless it is AUTO_GEAR. The throttle given to drivelineStep is clamp(c.throttle, 0, 1) x tractionControl x limiterCut. The surface is sampled at each wheel's contact point (4 surfaceAt calls a substep): fields a world leaves unset default to flat dry asphalt, and a NaN or negative grip becomes 0.
- Allocation test: I did both. Retained heap growth over 200,000 steps plus telemetry, with gc() reached through v8.setFlagsFromString('--expose-gc') and vm.runInNewContext('gc'), limit 2 MB (measured 127 KiB). And an identity check that every typed array in the state and in the telemetry record is the same object afterwards. Neither can see short-lived garbage, because V8 boxes doubles returned from functions it does not inline. Rule 4 is kept in the source: no literals, closures or spreads on the step's path.
- Not a bug, but surprising, in tyre.ts or the contract: below 2 m/s (brakes.ts ABS_MIN_SPEED) ABS passes the full pedal through, so the wheels lock for the last metres. Their deflection winds up to tyre.ts's 3-peak clamp, and at the stop, with no rolling to relax it, it acts as a spring and pushes the car back 4.2 cm. A real tyre gives back about 1 cm. Suggested fix in maxDeflectionX/Y: blend the clamp from 3 peaks at 1.5 m/s rolling speed to 1 peak at a standstill, because beyond the peak a stationary patch is sliding, not storing spring. I did not work around it; the test allows up to 5 cm.
- Latent, engine.ts:394-395: engineTorque subtracts the closed-throttle drag whichever way the shaft turns. That is safe for buildEngine electric motors (their regen table is 0 at 0 rad/s) and for combustion engines (driveline's ratchet). But an electric model whose friction[0] is not 0 (for example fixtureEngine with kind 'electric') drives the car backward from rest without limit. Fix: scale the drag by soft(omega, eps) so it always opposes the rotation.

## Bench, calibration, whole-car proof

- Skidpad (section 8): the ramp alone reads 3-4% low, because the last full lap averages half a lap's rise (0.7 m/s) under the speed it ends at. - Skidpad driver: pure pursuit from the rear axle (0.5 s look-ahead) plus an integral trim, never winding lock past the outer front tyre's peak slip angle.
